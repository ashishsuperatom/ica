Receivables — Semantic Model & Questions

As of 3 June 2026  ·  Source live MSSQL  ·  Currency ₹ crore (1 Cr = 1,00,00,000)

Three parts of one benchmark: the semantic model — the concepts we can compute on, each with its measures and the filters it can be sliced by; the questions those concepts answer; and the calculus — the pure form each question takes over the model. All three are meant to be grown; verified pieces are marked, candidates and data-blocked pieces are flagged honestly.

Part I

Semantic model — concept tree

Each concept is an entity — a real thing with a table behind it. A concept carries its relationships (how it joins to other entities, with verified coverage), the measures and metrics computable on it at a stated grain, the dimensions it can be sliced by, plus its identity, parameters and rules. Measures and filters are two facets of this — not the whole.

Time filter

A live snapshot of open balances as at 3 June 2026 — the latest bill in the ledger; every open trade-receivable is counted.

Derivation
  1. Take every open trade-receivable document (44,610 rows).
  2. Sum BALANCE → ₹256.3 Cr.
  3. Classify by the ledger's ageing field into within-terms / overdue buckets.
Show the query · developer
SELECT SUM(BALANCE)                          AS total_outstanding,
       SUM(CASE WHEN AgeingDays > 0
                THEN BALANCE ELSE 0 END)      AS overdue
FROM   dbo.rpt_db_outstanding_debtor;
Notes & judgements

Measure Summed on BALANCE, which floors advances at nil. CurrentBal nets 1,903 advances and understates by ₹23.4 Cr.  Collections policy §3 · mock

Ageing Uses AgeingDays (reflects credit terms); DueDate = bill date, so it is not used.

Coverage Whole population; exact sums. View the 44,610 underlying bills →

Exhibit 6 Analysis

So — is our receivable healthy or a problem?

Time filter Snapshot — as at 3 June 2026
Assessment
Concentrated, but current — the risk is who and where, not how old.
✓
Quality is high. 96% of the balance is current freight bills (Exhibit 5); of the overdue, 96% is under 60 days with barely ₹4.3 Cr aged past 60 days (Exhibit 1). Almost no bad-debt tail.
!
Concentration is real. 36 of 1,103 customers hold half the balance and two branches hold 28% (Exhibits 2–3) — one large default or a slow branch moves the whole position.
!
Discipline is uneven. The overdue clusters in Bangalore and a few branches (Exhibit 7), and ₹28.1 Cr is within 15 days of tipping overdue (Exhibit 8).

Net: healthy fundamentals with concentrated, near-term risk. The response is targeted — the specific overdue names and the Bangalore cluster — not a broad tightening.

Part III

Calculus — the pure form of a question

The calculus writes a question in the model's own vocabulary: nouns from the semantic model, a small fixed set of operators, and the parameters they take. It states what is asked — and, by its types, whether the model can answer it correctly. It carries no implementation: no tables, no columns, no SQL, no join keys. Those live in the TS program the calculus compiles to, kept separate. Pure means free of implementation, not free of parameters — a cutoff, a credit-term, a horizon belong to the calculus exactly as they belong to the semantic model. As the questions and the model grow, this grows with them.

Nouns — drawn from the semantic model typed; each carries grain, additivity, stock vs flow
measureoutstanding · overdue · bill_count — an aggregate over an entity; typed {grain · additive? · stock | flow}
metricdso · top-n share — a formula over measures; inherits their types
dimensionbranch · customer · ageing_bucket · document_type — a way to slice; typed by the entity it joins and its coverage
parametercredit_days · overdue_cutoff · horizon — a free, learned value; part of the calculus, never hard-coded in it
timeas-of · during · trailing — the temporal frame a measure is read in
Operators — the algebra a small fixed set; every question is a composition of these
Shape
select mname the measure or metric to compute
by d…group into slices along one or more dimensions
where φrestrict the population — a predicate over dimensions and measures
at τread at a time frame: as-of a date · during a range · trailing a window
top nrank the slices and keep the largest n
Compare
Δ(m, vs: base)m against a baseline — prior period · target · peer → value and direction
Explain
attribute(m, by: d)how much each slice of d contributes to m → ranked drivers
drill(m, path: d→d→…)decompose m step by step down to the evidence rows
Hypothetical
scenario(m, assume: ι)recompute m under an intervention ι — collect X, waive Y, tighten terms
simulate(m, over: τ, dynamics: Δ)evolve the state forward under stated dynamics
Judge
assess(m…, against: rubric)weigh several measures into a single verdict
Laws — correctness by construction the type system rejects the mistakes we actually made
  • A stock (outstanding, a balance) is read at: as-of; a flow (revenue, receipts) is read at: during. A stock ÷ flow like DSO needs both — the checker demands it.
  • by d is legal only if m is additive at d's grain; otherwise the sum is meaningless and the calculus refuses it.
  • where / by on a dimension is legal only if it joins m's entity — and its coverage travels with the answer (a 76% party-match is stated, never hidden).
  • A rule lives on the measure, not the query: outstanding already means balance floored at zero; overdue already means outstanding where ageing>0. Derived measures are reusable nouns.
  • The full expression is the concept's identity — ask it twice and you land on the same node. That is the reuse key.

Toward the enterprise simulation calculus the last three operators already point past reporting

State is every measure at a moment — the org as a vector. Dynamics are the rules that move it: a bill ages a day, a receipt clears, a truck depreciates, a lane reprices. Interventions are the levers: collect the top eight, tighten a party's terms, add a vehicle, re-rate a lane. scenario is state + one intervention, recomputed once; simulate is state + dynamics run forward over time. Give the calculus the full set of measures as state and the rules as dynamics, with parameters bound, and the same notation becomes an enterprise simulation calculus — the business run forward under assumptions, not only measured today. That is the next thing to grow, once the missing concepts in Part I (revenue, cash history, payables) have data behind them.
